**Design overview · 2 November 2026**

The **Triadic Brain**, or **Brain**, is the product we are working toward: a cooperative cognition system that keeps people in control of their tasks, data and participation. **Control Anchor is the intended everyday interface for a Sonya node**, not a replacement name for every part of the Brain. A Sonya node is a locally governed participant; a cooperative network connects such participants without giving one coordinator ownership of everyone else's work.

This is a selective, plain-language glossary, not an exhaustive specification, code inventory or completion report. Clarifying labels below are explanatory proposals. Existing repositories, modules, interfaces, source attribution and historical names are not renamed by this page.

**What runs here is much narrower:** this public repository contains an offline, synthetic reviewer component. It records supplied evidence and scripted review, preserves linked corrections, exports and reopens records, and exercises bounded integrity and recovery checks. It makes no model calls and provides no networked compute. See [the runnable scope](../README.md#what-this-repository-demonstrates-today), [validation](../VALIDATION.md) and [limitations](../KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md). The wider functions below are design responsibilities and research directions, not capabilities established by this demo.

## One product, distinct responsibilities

| Familiar name | Plain-language responsibility in the intended architecture | Important distinction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Triadic Brain / Brain | Coordinate useful analysis, challenge, presentation and continuity. | A functional analogy, not a claim of consciousness or biological equivalence. |
| Control Anchor | Let a person define goals, choose supported connections, set limits and inspect work on a Sonya node. | A setting displayed in a UI is not proof that execution enforces it. |
| Sonya / Sonya node | Connect permitted inputs and supported tools; host locally governed work. | Input connection and node ownership are related but distinct responsibilities. |
| Executive | Coordinate bounded tasks under the user's delegation. | A role is not a model, a session or a universal central authority. |
| Analyst, Challenge, Synthesis | Produce an answer, examine it independently where possible, and reconcile the contributions. | Naming three roles does not prove independent judgment or require three machines. |
| CoherenceLattice | Construct and organize candidates, claims, evidence relationships and alternatives. | Coherence alone does not establish truth. |
| Sophia | Evaluate a specified candidate and expose concerns, uncertainty and disagreement. | Evaluation does not grant new authority or replace required human judgment. |
| Atlas / Publisher | Present evidence, dissent and continuity; orient a task toward eligible prior work. | Presentation, retrieval and permission to use or retain a prior are different decisions. |
| UCC | Apply supported deterministic checks and finite procedures. | A correct calculation does not establish the correctness of its interpretation. |
| AEGIS | Apply technical admission and bounded defensive protections. | A concern or incident report does not authorize retaliation or broader access. |
| MORALware | Separate proposed normative review, evidence audit and protective guard responsibilities. | A staged design, not a moral oracle or proof of active product-wide enforcement. |
| Custody and event chronology | Preserve observations, decisions, revisions and uncertain outcomes. | Integrity and attribution are not factual truth, independent custody or permission. |
| PMR — Provenance Memory Reservoir | Retain and reuse eligible prior work under explicit rights and review. | Retention, retrieval, use, training and public sharing are separate choices. |

The intended triadic core relates candidate construction, evaluation and contextual presentation. The surrounding interfaces, controls, custody and resource owners keep those activities attributable and bounded. A useful end-to-end experience must connect these responsibilities; a glossary cannot establish that those connections work.

## Similar words that must not hide different functions

### TAC is not the admission aperture

**Telemetry Aperture Controller (TAC)** names a proposed function for controlling what is observed, at what resolution, for which purpose and with what disclosure and retention limits. It should balance useful evidence with privacy, accessibility and the burden of collection and review.

An **admission aperture**, more plainly an **admission gate screen**, asks whether stated consent, privacy, grounding and context conditions permit a proposed next step to be considered. Passing a screen is not itself execution authority. A gate screen is not a telemetry collector, and having one does not demonstrate the other.

### Atlas is not the optional Humanities Atlas

**Atlas / Publisher** is the evidence-presentation and prior-orientation role. The optional **432 Humanities Atlas**, also described as a contextual reference library, is a bounded knowledge/research resource. It is not the reporting role and is not a system for classifying people. A shared word does not make their evidence, purposes or permissions interchangeable.

### ANCHOR is not the Control Anchor interface

The proposed **ANCHOR guard kernel** belongs to MORALware's deterministic protective boundary. **Control Anchor** is the user-facing interface for a Sonya node. Their names overlap; their responsibilities do not. Neither an interface label nor a guard proposal establishes a qualified connection from the user's rule to a protected operation.

### TAF theory is not a runtime proxy

**TAF — Total Action Functional** names research into physical, informational and governance-related burdens. A bounded **review-action burden diagnostic** is a narrower proxy, not a measurement of the full theoretical objective. A useful account must identify which quantities were actually observed, which were estimated, the uncertainty and any burden shifted to others.

A formula, a software diagnostic, a formal result under stated assumptions and an empirical outcome are different kinds of evidence. No score can compensate for missing consent or override a resource owner's limits. This overview specifies neither a canonical formula nor an implemented optimizer.

## Looking back is part of cognition, not just storage

The intended system should learn from a task's history without silently rewriting it or treating every past result as reusable truth. These retrospective and continuity functions must remain distinct:

- **Question continuity:** keep the original question visible and detect when navigation, retrieved material or later work changes it.
- **Retrosynthesis:** work backward from an outcome, discrepancy or failed approach to propose alternative explanations and candidate next steps. Candidate construction belongs with analysis; displaying a candidate does not make Atlas its creator.
- **Prior orientation:** compare a new candidate with eligible earlier work. A proposed connection is not approval to store it or use it in a different task.
- **Scope-limited prior use:** distinguish background context from evidence supporting an answer; recheck rights and relevance at the point of use.
- **Post-use influence review:** examine how prior material affected the work. Word overlap alone is not proof of causal influence or semantic correctness.
- **Bounded exploration and stopping:** preserve unresolved questions, recognize missing evidence and stop or redirect under the accepted limits rather than continuing indefinitely.
- **Memory disposition:** recommend retention, further review, restricted handling or rejection without confusing a recommendation with an authorized memory write.
- **Correction, recovery and exit:** preserve the original and its linked correction, reconcile interrupted or uncertain effects, and support export of records the person is entitled to retain.

These are functions to preserve and evaluate, not a claim that this public demo implements a complete feedback or learning loop. Optional research such as **GUFT** representations and **AHA** cross-domain transfer should offer falsifiable candidates and allow “no adequate mapping.” Analogy is not evidence of equivalence, and retrieval-based reuse is not model training.

## A three-node goal, not a deployed network claim

A practical goal is a bounded demonstration across **three independently governed Sonya nodes**, with explicit analysis, challenge and synthesis responsibilities. Each receiving node would admit its own work under its owner's permissions and resource limits. The user-designated Executive would coordinate, not acquire ownership of the peers.

Three nodes are an engineering goal, not a requirement for first local usefulness or proof that three answers outperform one. A credible evaluation would compare the cooperative route with a suitable local baseline, assess correctness independently, retain disagreement and failures, and measure time, data movement and resource costs. Start with public or synthetic material and explicit stop conditions. Missing replies, cancellation and recovery need evidence too.

The goal is voluntary cooperation that expands agency. People may reasonably choose additional coordination overhead for local ownership, replaceable providers and practical exit. That choice must be informed; neither efficiency nor decentralization excuses hidden costs, coerced contribution or silent authority expansion. Sharing an artifact does not authorize compute donation, training or redistribution of its inputs. Revocation cannot guarantee retrieval of copies already disclosed.
