## Community ethos

> “We have to become compute commies to fight these guys.” — Thomas Prislac

The humor has teeth. Thomas is naming a class struggle over who owns computing’s productive capacity, who must rent access to it, and who gets to make the rules. The “oligarchs” are the controlling owners, major investors and executives whose concentrated power over chips, compute infrastructure, cloud services, model platforms and financing lets them shape the conditions under which everyone else builds and works. Ordinary technology workers are not the enemy.

Calling this a techno-feudal ruling class is an overt political criticism of that dependence. Labor and community knowledge sustain these systems; ownership gives a narrow class disproportionate power over their direction and the value they produce. A cheaper subscription does not resolve the power imbalance when the provider can set the terms and leaving is difficult. This criticism concerns structures of ownership and control; it does not allege criminality or secret coordination.

Class consciousness means recognizing that a developer’s API bill, a worker’s dependence on a platform and a community’s lack of computing infrastructure can be connected problems, rather than individual failures. Critical inquiry asks whose interests a supposedly inevitable technical arrangement serves, whose work sustains it and who has a meaningful say.

These concerns have a concrete institutional basis. The FTC’s January 2025 staff study of three cloud–AI partnerships described investment-linked cloud commitments and identified potential risks involving access to computing resources and switching costs. It did not determine that anyone had acted illegally. [FTC staff report](https://www.ftc.gov/reports/ftc-staff-report-ai-partnerships-investments-6b-study).

Our response is to organize useful alternatives: voluntarily shared resources, locally owned nodes and coordination accountable to the people doing the work. Participants should be able to inspect the software, help govern shared resources, choose providers and leave without surrendering their own machines or agency. Cooperation must not become another center of unaccountable control. Freedom has value even when it is not the cheapest benchmark result. We pursue it through working code, honest tests, documentation and contributions that give people more capacity to act together.

**Freedom-loving people are welcome to build with us.** Bring useful code, scrutiny, skills or spare capacity; participation requires no political affiliation.
