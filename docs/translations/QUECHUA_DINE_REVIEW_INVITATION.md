# Help translate Triadic Brain into Quechua and Diné Bizaad

**Status, 4 November 2026: invitation and preparation only. This page contains no Quechua or Diné Bizaad translation, and does not announce completed editions or fluent-human review.**

We welcome fluent speakers, translators and language educators who would like to help make two Triadic Brain papers understandable and accurate in Quechua and Diné Bizaad (Navajo). Help with one paragraph, a heading or a few technical terms is valuable. Reviewing an entire paper is not expected.

The conceptual and system design is by Thomas Prislac, Ultra Verba Lux Mentis. This invitation was prepared with AI assistance. Participation does not imply endorsement by a contributor's community, a language institution or an Indigenous nation.

## Language choices need fluent guidance

**Quechua:** the proposed starting point is Southern Quechua (Quechua sureño), with the Peruvian Ministry of Education's written conventions as a reference. Its [Southern Quechua library](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/minedu/informes-publicaciones/4548552-biblioteca-quechua-sureno) includes a writing manual and pedagogical vocabulary. Please help determine which variety and conventions would serve readers best, and identify differences between Chanka and Collao where they matter. Quechua has multiple regional varieties; a Southern Quechua edition would not serve every Quechua-speaking community equally. See the [Peruvian Ministry of Culture's overview](https://bdpi.cultura.gob.pe/pueblos/quechuas). No final variety choice or universal Quechua standard is claimed here.

**Diné Bizaad:** we seek fluent guidance on written language, technical explanations and terminology. The [University of New Mexico Navajo Language Program's references](https://navajo.unm.edu/books/) provide useful language resources, but do not certify this project's terminology or translations. Preserve tone marks, nasalization and other written distinctions. Where a technical equivalent is uncertain, retain the English term alongside an explanation for review rather than inventing an authoritative term.

These resource links are references, not affiliations or endorsements. The project does not ask contributors to disclose cultural knowledge or equate its architectural metaphors with community beliefs.

## Help with a small, concrete piece

1. [Open a public issue](https://github.com/pdxvoiceteacher/control-anchor-reviewer-demo/issues) or [submit a pull request](https://github.com/pdxvoiceteacher/control-anchor-reviewer-demo/pulls). Identify the paper, source commit, section and passage. Please indicate the written variety or convention used when relevant.
2. Provide the proposed wording and, if useful, a short explanation in English or Spanish. Mark uncertain terms and untranslated passages explicitly. A terminology suggestion alone is welcome.
3. Say whether AI assisted the contribution and whether a fluent human has reviewed that specific passage. AI checks and back-translation are not fluent-human signoff. A review of one passage does not certify an entire edition.
4. Tell us how you wish to be credited in the document: chosen name, pseudonym or no document credit. Public GitHub issues, pull requests and commits may still display account information and history; omitting document credit does not make a public contribution anonymous.

Please submit only material you are entitled to share publicly. Do not include private personal information, restricted community knowledge, credentials or confidential project material. No personal background, community membership or proof of identity is required. Follow the repository's [contribution guidance](../../CONTRIBUTING.md), [license scope](../../LICENSE_SCOPE.md) and existing notices. This invitation grants no new blanket license, access, execution authority or funding, and offers no payment or deadline.

## What needs especially careful review

- Preserve the difference between evidence and truth, uncertainty and zero, a proposal and a demonstrated capability, and a recommendation and permission to act.
- Check consent, refusal, revocation, privacy, custody and erasure language. A fluent sentence must not weaken the source's boundaries.
- Keep equations, code, identifiers, evidence codes, family IDs such as `F01`, and citation destinations unchanged. Translate their explanations and flag ambiguities.
- Keep the project names and metaphors recognizable without treating them as literal scientific mechanisms or borrowing culturally specific meanings without justification.
- Keep source and translated passages aligned, record unresolved terminology, and distinguish linguistic review from technical review.
